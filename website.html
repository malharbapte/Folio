From Brand Strategy to Digital Experience

No page was drawn until its reasons were written down and agreed. The brand became a map, a journey and a working prototype.

The flow I followed

Design Process & Workflow

Consultation came before every implementation step.

  1. DiscoverQuestionnaire, sales interviews, competitor review.
  2. MapEvery page of the old site, then the flow inside each page.
  3. VisualiseDrawn in Illustrator and exported as SVG.
  4. PrototypeBuilt as HTML, CSS and JS with Claude, hosted on GitHub.
  5. RefineSmall, frequent reviews, then motion added.

I went through every page of the existing site and rebuilt it as a flow chart in Miro. Seeing the navigation drawn out made the gaps obvious in a way clicking through never did. Then I mapped the flow inside individual pages: what a visitor sees first, what comes next, and what can wait. The order answers objections before it asks for anything.

The journey

Mapping the Customer Ownership Journey

A custom build takes nine to twelve months. A stock van takes about one.

Both start by understanding the customer and meet again at handover. Folding them into one sequence would either misdescribe the stock buyer or quietly demote them, and stock is a real reason people choose Outbound over a manufacturer with a waitlist.

  • Added: the showroom visit
  • Added: the weights and tow check
  • Added: pre-delivery inspection

The business had not named those three stages. The visit is the conversion moment the whole site exists to produce. The weights check is the strongest differentiator discovery turned up, so it sits before a buyer chooses anything. Pre-delivery inspection is a real dealership advantage that was invisible to the customer.

An exploration

Exploring Brand-led Graphic Elements

Eight patterns tested in the brand colours before one went on a tile.

The customer stories section mixes one review, three patterned tiles and four photographs. Every 4.5 seconds two tiles swap at the same time, and the old content closes into the centre.

Several of the patterns come from things the brand already owns: the chevron from the logo, the diamond lattice from the journey steps, and the soft curves from the ownership path. Three are shown on a tile, so they can be judged in place: offset chevrons, the diamond lattice and the stitched argyle.
